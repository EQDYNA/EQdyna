`2e1624d` -- median table row 1,006 bytes, longest row **32,331 bytes** (item

The investigation narrative behind the rows lives in `docs/BOARD_HISTORY.md`,
verbatim and unedited. It was **relocated, not deleted**, and every row that
had one points at it. That archive carries no priorities and no open items: an
obligation found there that no row here carries is a defect in this file.

A blank "Last checked" means never audited and stays blank until someone
actually runs the command. **A row's `Command` must be able to come out BOTH
ways** (rule 14a) -- a command that can only pass is not evidence, and two rows
were once found carrying commands that could never go red.

cell as `\|` (an unescaped one starts a new column mid-sentence; four rows in

**Perf-ledger wall-clock rows are only comparable within their tenancy.**
`docs/perf_ledger.jsonl` gained 66 rows on 2026-09-22 from two full
`testsys/run.py all` sweeps plus the GPU runs: the same 31 cells took 9413.8s
on a box shared with other agents' jobs (load average ~100 on 64 cores --
item 61's gate-d4f625e evidence) and 2904.6s / 3340.2s on quieter boxes (item
58's gate-a16cc98 evidence). Parity -- the actual pass/fail bound -- is
load-independent; wall clock is not, and rule 6's provenance fields (machine,
core count, compiler, git SHA) do not currently capture contention. Do not
compare a ledger wall-clock row against another without first checking they
ran under comparable load. **Rule 6a (written 2026-09-23) now states the
general form of this** -- `EFFECTIVE_CORES` admits a measurement and does not
make two measurements comparable; a paired, same-repetition, same-cpu-set
ratio is the only defensible perf claim on this box. It is a norm, not a gate:
a mechanical contention/box-state field in the ledger would have to be defined
first, and that is item 76, owner-scoped.
